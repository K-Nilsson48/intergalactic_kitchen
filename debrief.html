Intergalactic Kitchen: Rule inference and exploration task

Click to receive credit.

What are we trying to learn in this research?

When learning how an unfamiliar environment works, people often have to discover hidden combinations of rules through trial and error—much like figuring out which ingredients belong in a secret recipe. In this study, we investigate how different types of intermediate feedback (such as learning how many correct ingredients you included versus how many total changes are needed) and underlying structural rules affect how efficiently people explore and deduce unknown combinations.

Why is this important to scientists or the general public?

These questions are important for understanding how humans solve complex problems and make decisions under uncertainty. Comparing how humans use feedback and structural hints to narrow down possibilities with theoretical computer science limits helps researchers design better educational feedback systems and more efficient artificial intelligence learning algorithms.

What are our hypotheses and predictions?

We predict that participants will discover the secret recipes in fewer attempts when the feedback provides information about all five ingredients at once (such as total correct decisions or exact changes needed) compared to when the feedback only counts the correct ingredients currently inside the pot. Furthermore, we predict that participants who are informed of the underlying shape rule—that each recipe contains at most one item from each shape category—will use this structure to eliminate impossible combinations and solve the recipes in fewer guesses than participants who are not given this rule.
